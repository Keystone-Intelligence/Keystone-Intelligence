The Admin Burden

Every appearance generates paperwork.
Someone has to do it.

Irish solicitors on the Legal Aid panel carry administrative overhead that private practice doesn't. Claim forms, court diary management, mileage tracking, payment reconciliation: a practice management burden that takes a significant slice of every working week.

The volume

Fifty to eighty appearances a month

A firm with three fee-earners can handle fifty to eighty court appearances a month. Each one has to be recorded against the right certificate, and the claims go on different forms, including the LA1 for District Court criminal work, the LA10 for a solicitor's expenses, the CLA11 for prison visits and the Legal Aid Board's CF1 for District Court family law. The District Court claiming rules changed from 1 July 2026, and each form has its own format and data requirements.

The cost

Fifteen to twenty hours gone

Pulling case details, certificate numbers, fee codes and mileage from different places and entering them by hand. That's fifteen to twenty hours a month of fee-earner or administrator time on work that should be automatic.

Questions, Answered

What solicitors ask before they book.

How can AI reduce admin time for an Irish solicitor practice?

Most of the admin in a busy practice is rule-based: picking the right claim form for each case, assigning fee codes, calculating mileage at approved rates, tracking payments and writing up client calls. AI handles that work from data that already exists in your court diary and case files, and the solicitor reviews and signs off. In a three fee-earner practice that is typically around twenty hours of admin a month recovered, and post-call write-ups drop from twenty-five minutes to three.

Do I need to switch practice management systems to use Keystone's AI?

No. We build on top of the practice management system you already run, Keyhouse, Actionstep or whatever your firm uses. You keep your system, your data and the ways of working your staff already know. The automation sits above it and targets the specific bottleneck costing you the most time.

Can AI automate Legal Aid claim forms like LA1 and LA10?

Yes. Each form covers something different. The LA1 is the criminal legal aid claim form for District Court work, and the District Court claiming rules changed from 1 July 2026. The LA10 is for a solicitor's expenses, the CLA11 is for prison visits, and the CF1 is the Legal Aid Board's claim form for District Court family law. The system tracks which rules each case falls under, records every appearance, expense and visit against the right client and certificate, and generates the right form from existing case data, with mileage already filled in. The solicitor reviews and signs off, and the submission is logged for payment tracking. Forms that take 25–35 minutes to complete by hand are generated in under two minutes. We go into more detail, including the 1 July 2026 change, in our guide to AI for Irish solicitors.

Is Keystone's AI compliant with solicitor confidentiality and data obligations?

Compliance comes down to how a system is deployed, so we settle it in the first week of a build rather than after go-live. Privileged communications are not special category data under GDPR, but they carry professional obligations that overlap heavily with it. Every Keystone build runs on EU-hosted infrastructure, and we provide a written Data Processing Agreement with every engagement. Systems handling case data need a clear retention policy and an audit trail you can actually produce. In our call-actions build, every action carries a transcript reference. As the practice deploying the system, you remain the data controller. We cover the detail in our guide to AI and GDPR in Ireland.
