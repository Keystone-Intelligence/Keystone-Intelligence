AI Agent vs AI Chatbot: What Irish Businesses Actually Need

Jamie Walsh September 2026 6 min read

Most people use "AI agent" and "AI chatbot" interchangeably. Most vendors are happy to let that confusion sit, because a chatbot is far cheaper to build than an agent.

The distinction matters, because it decides what a business can expect back from the investment. This piece explains the difference in plain terms, then shows what it looks like in practice inside an Irish business.

The core difference, side by side

A chatbot answers questions. An AI agent does the work. It reads the incoming task, decides what needs to happen, acts inside your systems and hands back a finished result. Here is how the two compare on the things that actually affect a business.

AI chatbot vs AI agent
Compared on AI Chatbot AI Agent
Follows a fixed script Yes No. It reasons through the situation in front of it
Takes multi-step action No. It answers only Yes. It completes the task end to end
Connects to your systems Rarely, or only loosely Deeply. It reads and writes to Xero, Salesforce and your CRM
Handles one thing or many A single task Multiple related tasks in sequence
What it produces A reply A completed piece of work

Under the hood, an agent uses a large language model to read and reason, much as a chatbot does. The difference is everything wrapped around that model: the tools, the permissions and the connections into your business systems that let it act on what it has understood.

What this looks like in an Irish business, not in theory

Two systems Keystone has built make the difference concrete. In both cases a chatbot could have been bolted on instead. It would have answered questions and changed nothing about the workload.

EXAMPLE 01 · LEGAL PRACTICE

Client calls at an Irish solicitor practice

A chatbot can answer a client's question about the practice's opening hours. That is useful, and it is where a chatbot's job ends.

The agent Keystone built for an Irish solicitor practice works on the entire client call. It transcribes the call, decides what needs to happen next, and drafts a structured action list: what needs doing, who should do it and by when. Every action carries a transcript reference, so the fee-earner can verify anything later in a click. The fee-earner reviews, adjusts and assigns. Twenty-five minutes of post-call admin becomes three.

One replies. The other does the work.

EXAMPLE 02 · FOUNDER PRODUCTIVITY

A founder's inbox

A chatbot can tell a founder how many unread emails are waiting. The founder still has to read them.

Keystone's email triage agent reads the two hundred messages that arrive in a day, classifies each by priority, drafts replies to the routine ones in the founder's own voice, and escalates only what genuinely needs a decision. The founder sees the ten things that matter first, not a two-hundred-message inbox. The result is eight hours back every week, without losing visibility on anything that matters.

One reports. The other clears the inbox.

Why the distinction matters for what you should be paying

A chatbot is a relatively cheap, templated build. It is often available as an off-the-shelf tool that needs only light configuration.

An agent that reasons, integrates with your systems and takes multi-step action is a fundamentally different piece of engineering. It needs a data layer, connections into the tools you already run, and a review step for anything consequential. The price should reflect that.

If a vendor is quoting agent-level pricing for chatbot-level capability, that is worth questioning before you sign anything. Ask what the system will do inside your systems, not just what it will say.

For reference, Keystone builds at a fixed price between €1,000 and €20,000, agreed in writing before any work begins, depending on what the system has to do. The full breakdown is on the pricing page, and what AI actually costs an Irish business covers the payback side.

How to tell which one you actually need

The question is not which technology sounds more advanced. It is what you need finished at the end.

A chatbot may be enough if

  • The goal is answering common questions consistently
  • Nothing needs to change in any system once the answer is given

That is agent territory if

  • The goal is completing a process end to end
  • Data has to be extracted and a system updated
  • The output is finished work a person would otherwise do by hand

Most of the highest-value automation opportunities in an Irish SME sit in the second category. Reconciling invoices, clearing an inbox, turning a client call into assigned actions: none of that is solved by a better answer to a question. That is why Keystone builds agents rather than chatbots.
